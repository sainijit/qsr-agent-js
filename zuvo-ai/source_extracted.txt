Purpose: Source document for feeding into Gemini Pro to produce a curated Product Requirements Document (PRD), technical requirements, and implementation plan.Status: Concept / POC planning | Target market: Jaipur, Rajasthan, India
1. Executive Summary
ZuvoCloud is intended to become a multi-tenant SaaS platform for small and medium businesses, initially targeting businesses in Jaipur that already manage customer/order conversations primarily through WhatsApp. The core product should not attempt to replace WhatsApp. Instead, it should add an AI-powered operational layer on top of existing WhatsApp workflows: centralized visibility across multiple employee/worker WhatsApp numbers, structured business data, AI-assisted understanding and action, and an owner dashboard showing what is happening across the business.
The first objective is commercial validation, not maximum technical sophistication. The POC should demonstrate a realistic business workflow with multiple workers/numbers under one business tenant, AI-assisted message processing, business tools, and an owner dashboard.
2. Business Context
Founder: Jitendra, with extensive software engineering experience and Intel background.
Co-founder/family business operator: Jitendra's brother, currently available to help operate/build the business.
Existing company: ZuvoCloud Pvt Ltd / zuvocloud.com.
Initial geographic target: Jaipur, Rajasthan, because the brother will be based there and can support customer acquisition, onboarding, and local operations.
The business should initially focus on solving a concrete operational pain rather than building a generic AI chatbot.
Observed problem: many local businesses manage orders, customer inquiries, quotations, follow-ups, and other operations through WhatsApp, often across several employees/numbers.
Business owner pain: lack of centralized visibility into conversations, orders, leads, employee activity, pending actions, and business status.
Potential value proposition: keep WhatsApp as the employee/customer communication channel while ZuvoCloud provides intelligence, automation, structure, and owner-level visibility.
3. Product Vision
Build ZuvoCloud as a multi-tenant AI business operations platform where a business can connect multiple WhatsApp numbers/workers. ZuvoCloud ingests permitted WhatsApp business messages, identifies the tenant and worker, understands business intent, extracts structured information, invokes tenant-scoped business tools when appropriate, and presents an owner dashboard aggregating activity across the business.
The product should evolve from a WhatsApp intelligence/operations layer into a broader AI-native business operating system for SMEs.
4. Initial Target Customer
Geography: Jaipur first.
Ideal early customer: SME/distributor/trader/service business where customer communication and orders are already handled heavily through WhatsApp.
Business characteristics: multiple sales/support/order-handling employees; multiple WhatsApp numbers; owner wants visibility without forcing employees to abandon WhatsApp.
Avoid initially targeting businesses where WhatsApp is not a meaningful operational channel.
Initial customer discovery should validate which vertical has the strongest pain and willingness to pay.
5. Core Problem Statement
Customer messages and orders are fragmented across multiple WhatsApp numbers.
Owner cannot easily see what is happening across all five or more workers.
Important orders, leads, complaints, quotations, payments, and follow-ups can be missed.
Business information is trapped in unstructured conversations.
Owners may be forced to manually ask employees for status.
Existing WhatsApp workflows are familiar, so forcing a separate CRM/chat interface would create adoption friction.
6. Core Product Principle
Do not replace WhatsApp in the first product. Augment it. Employees should be able to continue working through WhatsApp while ZuvoCloud creates a structured, searchable, AI-assisted operational view for the business owner.

8. Example User Experience
Example inbound message:
“Sharma Traders ko 50 box 20mm pipe kal bhej dena.”
Expected AI extraction conceptually:
{"intent":"create_order","customer":"Sharma Traders","product":"20mm pipe","quantity":50,"delivery_date":"tomorrow"}
The AI should not directly invent business facts. It should use tenant-scoped tools to resolve the customer, product, inventory, pricing, credit, and order state.
9. Complex Reasoning Example
Example: “Sharma Traders ko last month wale rate pe 50 boxes bhej do, lekin agar credit limit cross ho rahi hai toh owner approval lena.”
Identify customer.
Retrieve relevant previous order/pricing.
Check current product/inventory.
Check credit status/limit.
Apply deterministic business rules.
If approval is required, create approval task/notification rather than silently placing an unauthorized order.
If all conditions are satisfied, create order through a controlled business tool.
Record the complete action/audit trail.
10. Functional Requirements
10.1 Tenant Management
Each business is a tenant.
Tenant has unique identifier.
Tenant configuration must be isolated.
Tenant has users/workers with roles.
Tenant has connected WhatsApp business numbers.
Tenant may have business-specific products, customers, pricing, policies, and rules.
Tenant-scoped AI context must never expose another tenant's data.
10.2 Users and Roles
Owner/admin role.
Worker/employee role.
Optional manager role for future.
Owner can view aggregated activity.
Workers should have only the permissions needed for their work.
Authorization must be enforced server-side, not only in the UI.
10.3 WhatsApp Integration
Use official WhatsApp Business Platform/Meta integration for production.
Receive inbound messages through webhooks.
Persist message metadata and content according to applicable privacy/data policies.
Identify tenant and worker from connected WhatsApp number.
Support multiple numbers under one tenant.
Maintain conversation/thread state.
Support outbound responses only through authorized WhatsApp APIs and tenant permissions.
Track delivery/status events where available.
Do not assume every message category is free; Meta pricing/category rules must be checked against the live India rate card before launch.
10.4 AI Message Understanding
Language and Hinglish-friendly understanding.
Intent classification.
Entity extraction.
Structured JSON outputs where appropriate.
Order extraction.
Lead/inquiry extraction.
Quotation-related intent.
Complaint/support intent.
Follow-up/task extraction.
Unknown/ambiguous intent handling.
Confidence/uncertainty representation where useful.
Escalation to human/owner when confidence is low or an action is risky.
10.5 Agent and Tool Calling
Use Hermes as the agent/orchestration layer if its final capabilities and licensing fit the selected implementation.
Keep LLM provider independent from the agent layer.
Use MCP-compatible business tools where practical.
Initial tools: get_customer, search_product, get_previous_order, check_inventory, get_price, check_credit, create_order, create_task, notify_owner.
All tools must enforce tenant_id and authorization.
Tools are the source of truth for business facts.
LLM is responsible for language/reasoning/tool selection, not authoritative business state.
Business rules such as credit limits, permissions, approval thresholds, and pricing rules must be deterministic in backend services.
10.6 Owner Dashboard
Business-wide activity overview.
Aggregate messages across all connected worker numbers.
Filter by worker.
Filter by customer.
Filter by conversation/status/date.
Orders detected/created.
Pending approvals.
Pending follow-ups/tasks.
Leads/inquiries.
Complaints/escalations.
Recent important conversations.
AI-generated business summaries/insights can be added after the basic dashboard works.
Dashboard must make it unnecessary for the owner to manually inspect five separate WhatsApp numbers to understand operational status.

12. Multi-Tenant Security Requirements
Never mix tenant context in prompts, tool calls, database queries, caches, or background jobs.
Every authenticated request must resolve tenant_id from trusted authentication/session context.
Never trust tenant_id supplied directly by an untrusted client.
Every database query involving tenant data must be tenant-scoped.
Every AI tool call must receive and enforce tenant context.
Redis/cache keys must include tenant identity where data is tenant-specific.
Background jobs must carry tenant context explicitly.
Audit security-sensitive actions.
Store API keys/secrets in a secret-management system, never in frontend code.
LLM requests should contain only the minimum context required for the task.
13. AI Architecture
Use an AI Gateway/LLM abstraction between Hermes and all model providers. The application should not hard-code one provider.
LLMProvider interface should support generation, structured output, and tool calling.
Initial cloud provider should be configurable.
Current preferred Phase-1 direction discussed: Gemini family for cost/performance, with Claude/OpenAI retained for benchmarking/fallback.
Do not make a final provider decision solely from generic benchmarks; evaluate on a representative set of real/anonymized WhatsApp messages.
Benchmark dimensions: intent accuracy, entity extraction, tool-call correctness, hallucination rate, incorrect actions, latency, token cost, Hinglish performance.
Local Qwen should be supported as a development/simple-task provider.
Future production optimization: route simple/high-volume tasks to smaller/local models and complex reasoning to a frontier cloud model.
Future private deployment: Qwen + OpenVINO/OVMS on customer or ZuvoCloud-managed hardware.
14. Recommended AI Routing Strategy
Tier 0: deterministic code/rules for trivial known states where no model is required.
Tier 1: small model for classification, extraction, simple orders, language detection, and routine messages.
Tier 2: stronger reasoning model for ambiguous/multi-step business tasks and owner-level analysis.
Tier 3: premium reasoning fallback only for genuinely complex tasks.
Do not send every WhatsApp message to an expensive frontier model.
15. Local Development AI
Primary development machine: MacBook configuration discussed as 32 GB RAM, 512 GB SSD, Core i7 (exact model identifier should be verified before final hardware documentation).
Older T480 with 8 GB RAM is sufficient for lightweight application development but is more RAM constrained.
Local small-model experimentation: Qwen3-class 0.6B/1.7B quantized models are appropriate; 4B may be possible on 32 GB RAM but inference speed depends on hardware/runtime.
Use Ollama or llama.cpp for simple local development.
Local model is a development/testing and simple-task option, not the initial production inference architecture.
Avoid running a large 7B/8B+ reasoning model on the 8 GB T480 while simultaneously running the full development stack.
16. Cloud Deployment — Phase 1
Preferred direction discussed: Google Cloud Platform, primarily because a simple Cloud Run + managed PostgreSQL architecture minimizes operational overhead during business validation.
Cloud Run for containerized API/backend/worker components.
Managed PostgreSQL via Cloud SQL.
Cloud Storage for files/artifacts if required.
Secret Manager for API credentials/secrets.
Redis/queue should be kept small initially; do not provision expensive managed infrastructure before traffic justifies it.
Do not deploy a dedicated GPU model server in Phase 1 unless there is a specific benchmark/demo requirement.
Use an India-region deployment where appropriate, with Mumbai/asia-south1 as the initial candidate.
Use Docker containers so the same application can later move to Kubernetes/GKE or another cloud.
Kubernetes should be deferred until scale/operational needs justify it.
17. Phase-1 Deployment Architecture
Conceptual deployment:
Internet/WhatsApp → API Gateway/Load Balancer → Cloud Run API → Tenant/Auth layer → PostgreSQL/Redis; AI Gateway → Hermes → configured LLM provider; background workers process asynchronous jobs.
Frontend can be deployed as a container/static web application.
API and worker should be independently scalable when needed.
AI Gateway should isolate provider-specific SDKs.
Use asynchronous processing for inbound message events where appropriate.
Implement idempotency for webhook/message processing.
Add structured logs, metrics, tracing, and audit events before real customer onboarding.
18. Cloud Cost Strategy
Do not rent a GPU server for Phase 1.
Initial cloud infrastructure target discussed: roughly ₹5,000–₹12,000/month as a planning envelope, excluding LLM API usage, Meta/WhatsApp charges, and unusual traffic; actual pricing must be recalculated from live provider pricing.
Track LLM cost separately from infrastructure.
Track per-tenant message count, tokens, model, latency, and estimated inference cost.
Set a development spending ceiling until paying customers exist.
Optimize model routing before optimizing infrastructure.
19. WhatsApp/Meta Cost Considerations
Meta WhatsApp Business Platform pricing is message/category dependent and can change; verify current India pricing at launch.
Service conversations/replies within applicable customer-service windows may have different/free treatment than outbound marketing, utility, or authentication messages.
Do not hard-code an assumed ₹0 Meta cost for the entire product.
Maintain usage accounting by tenant and message category.
Future billing should distinguish ZuvoCloud subscription, AI usage, WhatsApp/Meta pass-through or included usage, and premium features.
20. Business Rules and Safety
LLM must never be the authoritative source for inventory, pricing, customer credit, order state, permissions, or approval requirements.
Backend services and deterministic rules are authoritative.
High-risk actions should require explicit approval based on tenant rules.
AI should be able to ask for clarification instead of guessing.
Every consequential tool action should be auditable.
Support human override and correction.
Maintain an AIInteraction/ToolCall/AuditEvent trail sufficient to reconstruct why an action occurred.

24. Non-Functional Requirements
Multi-tenant isolation is a critical requirement.
API must be stateless where practical.
Webhook processing should be idempotent.
Background jobs should be retryable.
System should tolerate temporary LLM/API failures and retry/fallback appropriately.
No single LLM provider should be a hard architectural dependency.
Secrets must not be committed to source control.
Database backups must be enabled before real customer data is onboarded.
Production logging must avoid unnecessary sensitive message duplication.
The architecture should support future horizontal scaling.
The architecture should support future local/private inference.
25. Technology Direction
Backend: Python/FastAPI is the preferred direction for the AI-heavy POC.
Frontend: React/TypeScript.
Database: PostgreSQL.
Cache/queue: Redis or an appropriate lightweight managed/self-hosted option initially.
Agent: Hermes, subject to final capability/licensing validation.
Tool protocol: MCP-compatible tools where beneficial.
Cloud: GCP/Cloud Run direction for Phase 1.
Containers: Docker.
Local model runtime: Ollama or llama.cpp for development.
Future local inference: Qwen + OpenVINO/OVMS.
Authentication/authorization: production-grade identity with tenant-aware RBAC.
26. Future Architecture — Scale
Once usage reaches meaningful scale, evolve toward shared inference infrastructure rather than one model server per tenant.
Shared Qwen/OpenVINO/OVMS inference cluster for routine workloads.
Cloud frontier model fallback for complex reasoning.
AI Gateway routes based on task complexity, cost, latency, tenant policy, and availability.
Kubernetes/GKE only when operational scale warrants it.
Dedicated database or dedicated deployment can become an enterprise tier.
Private/on-premise/edge deployment can be offered to customers with privacy or network requirements.
27. Future Private/Edge Product
A future ZuvoCloud Edge product can leverage the team's Intel/OpenVINO expertise.
Customer-hosted or ZuvoCloud-managed local inference.
Qwen-family model served using OpenVINO/OVMS where hardware/model compatibility supports it.
Cloud reasoning fallback can remain optional.
Customer data processing can be kept closer to the customer's environment, subject to WhatsApp/Meta integration constraints and applicable compliance requirements.
Potential enterprise differentiator: private AI business operations rather than only a generic cloud AI wrapper.

30. POC Acceptance Criteria
At least one tenant can be created.
At least five workers/users can belong to one tenant.
Multiple WhatsApp numbers can map to the same tenant.
Inbound messages are correctly mapped to tenant and worker.
Messages are persisted and displayed.
AI can classify/extract at least the initial supported intents.
AI can call at least three tenant-scoped business tools.
Tenant A cannot access Tenant B's customers/messages/orders through API, UI, cache, background job, or AI tools.
Owner dashboard aggregates all worker activity.
Owner can filter activity by worker/customer/status.
A business rule can prevent an unsafe/unauthorized order and create an approval task.
LLM provider can be swapped through configuration/provider abstraction.
Local development can use a small local model without changing business logic.
Cloud deployment can run without a dedicated GPU.
Basic audit logs exist for AI/tool-driven business actions.
